Me and Game Development

I began my college career by majoring in animation and gaming at Mt. San Antonio College where my curriculum focused more on animation. Creating animations was enjoyable, but not something that I was passionate about. I found my passion when I took the Intro to Gaming course where I was introduced to programming in the Unity engine. After that, I changed my major to Computer Science where I began to learn general programming concepts that I found extremely enjoyable. From then on, I focused on taking computer science and math courses and I graduated from Mt. SAC community college with an associate degree in mathematics, then I transferred to California State Polytechnic University, Pomona.

I studied for 2 years at Cal Poly Pomona and earned a bachelor’s degree in computer science, but I found the most valuable time I spent at the university was my final year when I joined the CPP game development club. The CPP game dev club consisted of students that would pitch an idea for a game that they wanted to create, and others would join the team of the pitch they liked the most. Throughout the semester, the team would meet up to collaborate and create a fun and unique game. Meeting up at the end of every week was probably the highlight of my final year at Cal Poly Pomona. Although I did not pitch any games or lead any teams, I worked hard to learn from my team lead so that we could create fun experiences with our games.

Using what I learned from the game dev club, I was able to create a VR game with a team of 4 people for my senior project. A senior project is an extracurricular course where students work on ideas outside of the core classes of their major program with little guidance from a professor. My senior project focused on creating an educational game that illustrated a concept from one of the courses offered at CPP. Developing this game was an interesting experience that helped me to work more with others as well as give some insight into developing a game that can be fun but also educational.

My time making games in college was all extracurricular activities without formal guidance from the college, but I learned a lot about game development with my peers. My time with the game development club at CPP taught me how to collaborate with others to create fun and interesting games, create core movement mechanics, create simple UI and menus, create simple AI enemies and enemy spawners, create levels and level design, and I learned the process of creating games from start to finish.

My favorite part of making games is collaborating with other people to make core mechanics that are fun and satisfying. Now, I am learning to create games using Unreal Engine.
